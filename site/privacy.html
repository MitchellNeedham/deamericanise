Privacy policy

The deamericanise extension for Chrome, Edge and Firefox. Last updated 11 November 2026.

The extension sends nothing anywhere. It reads the pages you visit so that it can rewrite them, and it does this inside your browser, on your device. It makes no network requests, and it has no analytics, no advertising and no cookies. The source code is public, so anyone can check this.

What the extension reads

  • The text of each page you open, in every frame, and the title, alt and placeholder text of its elements. This is the text it rewrites.
  • The markup around that text, such as the language the page declares and the parts it marks as not to be translated.
  • The address of each page, to check whether you switched the extension off for that site.
  • The position of your pointer, when highlighting is on, to show what a change replaced.

None of this leaves your device, and none of it is kept after you leave the page.

What the extension stores

It stores your settings: the dialect, the mode, the categories you switched on or off, the confidence levels, whether to keep the original measurement, how it decides a page is American, whether highlighting is on, the rules you switched off, and the sites you switched it off for. You can see and change all of them on the extension's settings page.

The settings are kept in your browser's storage for extensions. If you sync your browser, it copies them to your other devices through your Google, Microsoft or Mozilla account. Your browser's maker handles that copy under its own privacy policy. The list of sites you switched off is the only setting that says anything about your browsing.

Sharing

The developer never receives any data from the extension, so there is nothing to sell, share or transfer.

Permissions

  • Storage keeps your settings.
  • Access to every site lets the extension rewrite text on any page you read. Your browser may describe this as reading and changing your data on all websites.
